# NELIOTA rate context for the Artemis II flashes

Date checked: 10 October 2026

Primary source: Liakos et al., "NELIOTA: New results and updated statistics after 6.5 years of lunar impact flashes monitoring," *Astronomy & Astrophysics* 687, A14 (2024).

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202449542
- Publisher PDF: https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2024/07/aa49542-24.pdf
- ArXiv record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19613

## Decision-bearing values

The paper reports 192 validated flashes in 283.4 observing hours, divided into 145 sporadic flashes over 237.8 hours and 47 stream-associated flashes over 45.6 hours. Table 2 gives the validated sporadic normalized detection rate as `1.96e-7 flashes km^-2 h^-1`. Under the paper's homogeneous-bombardment assumption, Table 3 extrapolates the validated sporadic sample to `7.4 meteoroids h^-1` over the full lunar surface. The validated stream value is `12.6 h^-1`.

The paper's sampled physical regime is not all lunar impacts. Its abstract reports that more than 75 percent of the inferred impactors had masses of 1 to 200 grams and radii of 0.5 to 3 centimeters under the authors' adopted luminous-efficiency assumptions.

## Proper use

The NELIOTA whole-Moon value makes five accepted Artemis events during a nearly hour-long eclipse plausible in broad order. It argues against treating the count alone as evidence of an anomalous rate.

It does not calibrate Artemis II. NELIOTA and Artemis have different brightness thresholds, bandpasses, detection methods, viewing geometries, live-time definitions, and confirmation rules. Artemis lacks a limiting magnitude and human detection function. Therefore:

- do not divide or subtract the two counts as though they came from one sampling process;
- do not run a Poisson significance test using `7.4 h^-1` as an Artemis expectation;
- do not treat numerical proximity as independent confirmation of every crew event; and
- keep the result as contextual plausibility evidence only.

## What this changed

The strongest framing is no longer "five mysterious flashes may reveal a surprising impact rate." It is:

> Five is a credible, broadly plausible detected count obtained from an unusually favorable human vantage point. The scientific gap is not the numerator. It is the absent human and camera measurement function needed to turn that numerator into a calibrated rate.
