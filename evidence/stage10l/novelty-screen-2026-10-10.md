## What the screen found

WIRED presents about 800 GB, 11,000 full-resolution images and videos, transcripts, notes, and the browsable raw-data archive as one intuitive release object. NASA similarly announces more than 800 GB and more than 11,000 images and videos. The official archive-plan paper does distinguish source, PDS-reformatted raw, and derived or calibrated processing levels, so the existence of multiple representations is not itself novel.

The exact searches returned no public result reporting the Stage 10L cohort counts, the 10.534626 product/action ratio, or action-level reconstruction as a sampling denominator. The closest authoritative material explains processing levels and stable NASA IDs but does not quantify how those representations and exposure brackets compress into physical camera actions.

## Decision

Keep the four-unit result as the strongest current public-analysis candidate, sharpened around the exact complete-action cohort. Its differentiated contribution is not “PDS has processing levels”; it is the verified consequence that one defensible cohort can be counted as 22,818 files, 7,606 exposures, or 2,166 actions, plus a demonstrated activity facet that returns products while recovering zero complete actions.

This is a bounded public-search screen conducted three days after release. Search absence does not prove priority, novelty, or that no other analyst has reached the result. Before publication, rerun a current screen and check relevant PDS/NASA technical discussion, conference materials, code repositories, and any newly indexed analyses.
