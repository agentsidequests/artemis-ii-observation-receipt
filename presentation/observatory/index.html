Is Sunset 20% or 52%?
First, tell me what counts.

A target name is a label on an archive record, a little like a name on a photo album. Here, “Sunset” is one recorded name. This comparison starts with 7,606 target entries in our original reconstructed set of camera records, not the entire mission archive. Pick a rule to see how the answer changes.

What changed, and why

A blank name is still an unknown.

1,553 entries are recorded as Sunset. Another 4,193 have no target name. Keeping the blanks in the total gives 20.418%.

The real Moon.

This is an archived crew-camera browse image. Zoom in and move around. Then notice the distinction: seeing detail is one thing; knowing exactly what a file represents is another.

Image description, provenance and what zoom cannot tell us

The frame shows a densely cratered gray surface. Craters overlap, some bright streaks radiate across darker regions, and the top of the Moon curves against black sky. The image is a processed browse representation, not a calibrated measurement used by this interaction.

Zooming changes only how large these stored pixels appear. It adds no detail to the file and does not establish the cause, age, brightness or exact location of a feature. There are no analytical hotspots or inferred impact sites.

05 / The most tempting leap

A flash is a beginning.
Not yet a rate.

The crew's retained flash reports make you want to ask how often the Moon gets hit. But first: what had a chance to be noticed, recorded and kept?

No new flash is identified here. Event brightness, human detection thresholds, precise locations, crater confirmation and calibrated lunar impact flux remain unsupported by this analysis. There is deliberately no “catch the flash” game pretending to measure them.

What this could make possible

Make the next question
easier to answer.

NASA already preserves rich provenance and mission context. Our recommendation is a small bridge from those records to a declared research task, with a clear test of whether the bridge is worth maintaining.

A

For an existing camera or machine-learning task

Show what is related
and what gets counted.

Pilot a versioned relationship table linking files, capture keys and reconstructed sequences. Attach the chosen train/test boundary, selection rule and weights. Keep uncertain identities visible.

Concrete deliverables and acceptance

A versioned relationship ledger, reproducible split assignment, selection summary and weighting summary for one declared task. A qualified archive/ML reviewer checks semantics; a separate consumer reproduces the result. Name a maintainer and measure the cost of review and updates against actual reuse. This is a proposed pilot, not a NASA commitment.

B

For a flash-measurement task

Establish what one
interval could measure.

For one frozen interval, inventory coverage, exposures, viewing conditions, timestamp meanings and available calibration. Missing sensitivity or event coverage means reporting the limit, not estimating an impact rate.

Concrete deliverables and acceptance

A coverage/timing/calibration feasibility inventory and explicit missing-data list for one bounded interval. A qualified lunar-impact specialist reviews measurement feasibility before any physical-rate or photometry claim. Independent replay and maintenance value must be established before expansion.

Two separate pilots. The camera task need not wait for flash calibration. Neither proposal claims a new archive standard, global novelty or agency endorsement.

Plain-language glossary and ways to use this page
Archive
A collection of stored files and the records describing them.
Metadata
Information about a file, such as a camera identifier, time or recorded target name.
Capture key
A native identifier used in this reconstruction. It is not automatically a certificate of a unique physical exposure.
Reconstructed action or sequence
A group inferred using our declared metadata rules, with known order sensitivity.
Representation
A stored form associated with a capture key, such as a source NEF or a processed TIFF.
Denominator
The total you divide by. Changing it can change a percentage even if its numerator, the counted part, stays the same.
Calibration
Information needed to connect a recorded signal to a physical measurement.
