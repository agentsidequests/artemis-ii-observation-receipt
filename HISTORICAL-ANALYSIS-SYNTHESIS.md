# One planned Artemis II Sunset event spans at least 4,290 context products

**Private decision brief : 10 October 2026**

**Evidence baseline:** verified Stage 0–11M receipts, including the Stage 6B specialist correction, Stages 7A–9B scientific and representation tests, the Stage 10 archive-structure and readiness checks, and the Stage 11 replication, paired-window, plan-realization, connected-context, unit-choice, protocol-weighting, unit-sensitive-prominence, protocol-context-alignment, implicit-weight-factorization, rank-reversal-attribution, byte-rank-factorization, deterministic split-receipt, and implementation-diverse receipt-audit canaries

**Publication status:** private analysis; no global novelty or scientific-discovery claim

**Current lead after the bounded research pass:** the release is not one evaluation-ready dataset with one size, sample count, label population, or measurement level. One independently planned eight-minute Sunset block can be counted as 2,535 products, 845 exposures, 169 bracket actions, 24 conditional bursts, or one scheduled event. Yet a conservative transcript-bracketed connected context contains 4,290 products, 1,430 exposures, and 286 actions while the scheduled-event count remains one. Reconstructing the observation more completely makes the relevant archive footprint larger without increasing the event-level sample size. Flattening the graph also changes implicit weight: this context is 13.204% of cohort actions, 18.801% of products, and 22.785% of bytes. Across active plan windows, changing from actions to products reverses 38 strict pairwise size comparisons; changing from actions to bytes reverses 59. “Largest observation” is therefore not unit-neutral, although Sunset remains first under all three totals. The weighting is operationally structured: five-exposure actions are 35.455% of the deduplicated plan-associated action union but 47.794% of its flat products, and their occurrence aligns with camera and contiguous time regimes. This brief records the evidence and limits; it is not a public-analysis draft.

## The conclusion

NASA's “more than 800 GB” statement has an explicit denominator: Preliminary Science Report Table 4.1 reports 11,591 source files totaling 814.51 GB. The preserved Atlas snapshot answers a different question: 32,133 downloadable products total 2.526790075510 TB after source files are converted, multiplied into additional representations, regrouped, transcribed, or embedded into other metadata. The earlier 833.887936412-GB subtotal resemblance is not the documented accounting basis and is superseded.

The mismatch is structural, not merely rounding. Current Atlas holds 10,329 Nikon capture identities in three equally sized source, raw, and processed collections:30,987 products before Orion or audio. The official crew-source ledger contains 10,407 files. Sixty-eight iPhone images are explicitly pending a later archive update; ten Nikon source-to-current count differences remain unexplained. Orion's 28-still difference is entirely Cab Cam 2. Audio follows another mapping again. These are provenance and release-state differences, not evidence that data are lost, defective, withheld, or erroneous.

The evidence unit changes again inside the camera archive. The expanded six-pattern census finds 2,166 complete exposure-bracket actions spanning 7,606 raw exposures. Twenty-five actions cross activity facets. Four occur outside Flight Day 6, so the earlier four-pattern Flight-Day-6-only generalization is superseded. In the strongest stress case, a Discussion #4 activity filter returns 84 products representing 28 exposures but recovers zero complete camera actions; 14 companion exposures sit under Crew Choice. Product counting overstates independent evidence while facet filtering can under-recover the physical action at the same time.

The complete-action cohort makes the scale of the unit collision exact. Every one of its 7,606 exposure identities has one source, one raw, and one processed Atlas product: 22,818 downloadable products representing 2,166 complete camera actions, or 10.534626 products per action. This is not an archive-wide inflation or quality claim; it is the strongest verified demonstration that a file count and an observation-action count can differ by more than an order of magnitude before any image analysis begins.

That structure creates a concrete AI evaluation hazard. Under a frozen hypothetical independent 80/20 product split, an expected 1,930.148 of the 2,166 verified action groups:89.111%:place related products on both sides. Grouping the three representations by exposure is still insufficient: independently splitting exposure identities crosses an expected 1,146.048 actions, or 52.911%, because bracket siblings remain divided. Splitting at the reconstructed action level crosses zero of these exact groups by construction. This is an expected group-crossing model, not observed leakage or an allegation about any existing pipeline; real tasks may require still broader grouping by scene, target, time, or source episode.

The broader-group caveat is now quantified rather than boilerplate. Complete actions themselves often arrive in rapid same-camera runs. If a later task conditionally treats actions separated by no more than five seconds as one related temporal cluster, 1,656 of 2,166 actions belong to multi-action clusters; independent 80/20 assignment of actions puts an expected 1,239.004 actions:57.202%:inside clusters crossing the split. The result ranges from 4.046% under a one-second rule to 86.193% under a thirty-second rule. At five seconds, 290 of 306 multi-action clusters carry one activity value across all admitted exposures, and 132 of 139 clusters containing any target carry one target value. That facet concordance is not independent:the labels are time-derived, partly curated, and already known to fracture:so it supports structured capture runs without proving semantic similarity. No threshold is promoted to scene truth. The decision consequence is that action identity is the exact minimum grouping key, while the real task must define the broader dependency boundary before training.

That dependency hierarchy is now executable rather than merely descriptive. A deterministic 10,072,026-byte relationship manifest maps every one of the 22,818 cohort products to its capture identity, private reconstructed-action key, and each conditional temporal-cluster key. It intentionally assigns no train, validation, or test membership. The next model task must decide what generalization it is testing before selecting which relationship key:or a broader task-native key:controls the split.

The proposed split receipt has now been tested against that hierarchy. Using one frozen hash rule, seed, and 80/20 target, product-key hashing crosses 1,937 actions. Action-key hashing crosses zero actions but still divides 157 of 816 conditional five-second groups, placing 1,216 actions inside crossed outer groups. Five-second-key hashing crosses neither declared layer. Nine deliberately bad variants fail. This converts the recommendation from prose into an executable canary without making five seconds a semantic scene boundary or claiming that any real pipeline leaked information.

A second local implementation now replays the serialized source, assignment, receipt, algorithm, and metrics without importing Stage 11L code. It reproduces every key count and rejects 11 serialized-artifact mutations at the required checks. This is partial implementation diversity, not independent human replication: both paths share the declared source, relationship semantics, repository, and authoring episode.

One microcase makes the stakes visible. The largest conditional five-second cluster is a single-camera Sunset sequence lasting 143.61 seconds. It contains 82 complete five-exposure actions:410 captures multiplied into 1,230 archive products totaling 75.056509295 GB. Every admitted row carries the same `24_Sunset` activity and `Sunset` target labels. If its 82 actions were independently assigned 80/20, the cluster would cross the partition with probability `99.999998869%`.

The independent crew transcript turns that from a merely convenient metadata burst into a supported observation episode. Thirty-five timestamped utterances fall inside the exact camera interval: 34 from Hansen and one from Wiseman, continuously describing the Sun, Moon, glow, plumes, and streamers. Related speech brackets the sequence just 2.084 seconds before it begins and 0.013 seconds after it ends. The transcript still does not prove that the 410 exposures are visually redundant, identify who operated camera `3500015`, validate the archive labels, or show actual model leakage. It establishes the missing human-execution context: a short, coherent observation episode can masquerade as more than a thousand flat records.

The episode is larger and structurally messier than the first camera alone. Exhaustive interval overlap finds three nested runs from a second camera, `3920019`, adding 780 products from 260 exposures and 52 actions while 24 of the Sunset-observation utterances occur. Combined, the episode yields 2,010 products from 670 exposures and 134 actions totaling 177.543697399 GB. Those 144 seconds account for 8.809% of all products and exposures, 6.187% of actions, and 10.924% of bytes in the verified complete-action cohort. Both cameras carry the `Sunset` target, but their records span `24_Sunset` and `h_Observation Break`. That does not make either activity label wrong. It demonstrates why neither a single-camera cluster nor an activity facet is a safe universal proxy for the real observation episode.

The stronger boundary comes from the mission plan rather than the observed concentration. The outgoing request schedules Sunset from `00:36` to `00:44 UTC`; the next planned request begins at exactly `00:44`. At `00:40:28`, the transcript says the crew is still on Sunset for about four more minutes, and at `00:41:31` confirms that the camera is set for the corona. Every complete-action cohort row fully inside that fixed window carries the Sunset target label, although the Data User Guide warns that target tags were manually assigned for only a subset and not thoroughly verified.

Inside those eight minutes are 2,535 downloadable products representing 845 exposures and 169 complete five-exposure actions across two cameras, two activity facets, and 24 conditional five-second same-camera clusters. Their 200.901668231-GB retrieval footprint is not 200.9 GB of source capture: it decomposes into 17.257373901 GB of source NEFs, 46.133066720 GB of raw TIFFs, and 137.511227610 GB of processed TIFFs. The current archive footprint is therefore 11.641497 times the source bytes for the same fixed block. Sunset ranks first by products, actions, and bytes among all 36 planned windows that contain admitted complete-action rows.

That produces the most consequential result of the tranche. Sample size is task-conditioned: the same block is `2,535 products → 845 exposures → 169 actions → 24 conditional bursts → 1 scheduled event`. None of the first four counts is automatically an independent-sample count. If the claimed deployment target is a new Sunset event, the event-level sample size in this mission is one. There are two ways to allocate one whole event:train or test:and zero ways to put distinct Sunset events in both. Splitting within it can support a narrower time-forward, HDR, representation, or retrieval task, but cannot estimate new-event generalization.

A second, independent evaluation hazard survives even after the unit hierarchy is respected. Among 3,413 source exposures with nonblank target tags in the verified complete-action cohort, 19 of 23 target classes occur on only one camera and account for 807 exposures. The Data User Guide independently identifies the Z9 as the primary eclipse camera; 593 of its 734 labeled exposures carry `Sunset`. The deliberately simple metadata rule “predict Sunset iff Z9” scores 67.741% accuracy against a 54.498% majority baseline, with 65.302% balanced accuracy and 80.790% precision:but only 38.184% sensitivity. The countercase matters: Sunset spans two cameras and the Z9 also carries non-Sunset labels, so camera identity is neither necessary nor sufficient.

This is a prospective confounding canary, not an observed shortcut. It does not validate the target tags, show that pixels reveal the camera, prove that a model would use camera signatures, or estimate a performance effect. It changes the minimum evaluation contract: any later target classifier should report a camera-only baseline, expose unsupported camera-target cells, and separate camera/event-stratified evaluation from ordinary random splitting. Camera balancing cannot manufacture the second Sunset event that Stage 10T shows is missing.

The label population itself is more selected than that canary initially made visible. Target tags are present for only 3,413 of the 7,606 source exposures in the complete-action cohort:44.872%. Across 45 scheduled-activity values, 17 have zero target-tag coverage, eight have complete coverage, and 20 are partial. `24_Sunset` is 1,294/1,294 tagged, while `12_Orientale Basin (2/2)` is 0/399 and `07_Whole Moon` is 3/456. An explicitly in-sample activity-majority lookup classifies whether a tag is present at 87.484% versus a 55.128% global-majority baseline; Cramér's V is 0.820465.

This does not prove a formal missing-not-at-random mechanism or reveal why an analyst tagged a frame. It does prove that tag presence is observably nonuniform. Stage 10U therefore remains a valid canary inside the tagged subset, but its camera-target association can reflect both acquisition and curation structure. A future target model must audit label presence before it interprets a camera-only baseline, and blank tags cannot silently become negative examples. The plan-bounded Sunset result survives because its time window was selected from the mission plan without a target filter; unanimous Sunset tags inside it are corroboration, not the selection rule.

Byte weight also fails as a proxy for measurement readiness. Processed TIFFs account for 67.932% of the 1.625281365343-TB complete-action cohort and 68.447% of the 200.901668231-GB fixed Sunset block. Yet the Data User Guide says that no calibration was applied and no calibrated crew-camera products are provided. The processed files are explicitly uncalibrated, interpreted outputs produced with Nikon's proprietary demosaicing, default camera settings, sRGB, and autoscaling; their colors cannot be completely verified or quantified without additional information, calibration, and testing.

That does not make processed data defective. It makes representation choice task-conditioned too: source NEFs preserve native pixels and metadata, raw TIFFs expose the Bayer array in an open archival form, and processed TIFFs supply a human-viewable vendor interpretation. None alone supplies instrument-independent physical units or quantitatively verified color. The largest byte tier is therefore neither the largest independent sample nor automatically the strongest measurement tier.

The primary sources make the corrective nuance essential. NASA says the image sequences were intentionally designed for multiple settings, HDR, mosaics, and observation of an evolving corona. This is intentional structure, not a demonstrated archive defect. These related records are not simply clutter to deduplicate: removing them can erase the scientific structure, while treating them as independent can invalidate an evaluation. The correct move is to name the prediction target and preserve the corresponding hierarchy.

The strongest current insight is therefore methodological but consequential: **“How much of the Artemis II archive did you analyze?” has no meaningful answer until the analysis names its ledger, snapshot date, and reconstruction unit.** Source files describe mission acquisition, archive products and bytes describe retrieval burden, capture identities describe exposures, and reconstructed actions describe observation evidence. Any AI or human pipeline that treats those as interchangeable can simultaneously triple-count representations and split one physical observation across search categories.

The Stage 6A Orientale reframe did not survive specialist-literature pressure as a quantified headline. Its 10–60× arithmetic is numerically correct, but it divides unlike quantities: a short horizontal baseline used to compute LOLA roughness and a task-specific object-recognition bound derived from one rille observation. LOLA aggregates many short-baseline measurements into regional map patterns, so agreement between crew language and a roughness map does not show that the crew sensed individual hectometer-scale terrain features.

NASA's real result remains valuable: around Orientale, crew-described regional texture corresponded qualitatively with a LOLA roughness map rather than a Diviner 1–2 m rock-abundance map. But NASA already publishes that interpretation. The primary texture-psychophysics paper also remains relevant only as mechanism plausibility for controlled hair-like line textures; it does not validate lunar-terrain perception.

Stage 7A now supplies the first held-out non-Orientale signal. Before reading LOLA values, the Testbed froze Glover's semantic contrast that Vavilov was rougher than Hertzsprung. Equal-size 20 km regions agree at all three predeclared roughness baselines: Vavilov's median slope is 1.79× Hertzsprung's at 57 m, 2.40× at 225 m, and 3.05× at 560 m. All nine combinations of 10, 20, and 30 km regions across the three scales preserve the expected direction.

That is consequential evidence, but it is not yet the article. The same astronaut supplies the descriptions; the two craters have visibly different morphology and likely age and geologic context; center disks are only coarse location proxies; and illumination, albedo, viewing geometry, and optical tool remain uncontrolled. The result shows cross-instrument directional concordance. It does not show that language contributes information beyond obvious morphology or that humans sensed terrain below object-resolution limits.

Stage 7B tested that promised continuation before opening any additional instrument values. Ten plausible transcript episodes were coded. Zero are simultaneously new, held out, directly comparative, target-localized, morphology-matched, control-frozen, non-Orientale, and on the shared LOLA roughness endpoint. The apparent sample size disappears once the analysis refuses to recycle Stage 7A, readmit Orientale, invent coordinates for diffuse regions, or pool reflectance and geologic-age claims with slope.

That is a verified design stop, not a failed signal. Stage 7A still does not yet show semantic information beyond crater morphology, and Stage 7B shows that this transcript alone cannot answer the generalization question cleanly. Its strongest surviving cross-axis candidate was Hansen's direct comparison of Glushko and Ohm ray brightness. Reiner Gamma's albedo uniformity remained secondary. Neither could be counted as more LOLA roughness evidence.

Stage 8A has now tested the Glushko/Ohm claim under a prospectively frozen normalized-reflectance proxy. Before value access, the Testbed fixed Hansen's “very similar, Glushko slightly brighter” judgment, official crater centers, equal crater-normalized annuli, independently named Aristarchus-directed ray axes, symmetric local-background flanks, the LROC WAC 566 nm band, thresholds, and nine robustness cells. The named Glushko sector is 2.014% darker than its local flanks; Ohm is 0.106% darker. Neither reaches the required positive 5% contrast, Glushko does not exceed Ohm, and `0 / 9` robustness cells meet either the complete contrast-plus-similarity oracle or the required direction.

This is the most informative kind of negative: the predeclared treatment failed without being rescued after pixel access. It does not refute what Hansen saw. It shows that a static, photometrically normalized, single-band local-contrast map is not an adequate stand-in for that reported visual comparison under the frozen named-axis geometry. Actual viewing geometry, illumination, spectral stimulus, visual adaptation, optical path, selected rays, substrate, and scene context remain materially different. The Glushko/Ohm reflectance lane therefore stops without post-outcome retuning rather than switching bands or redrawing sectors.

Stage 8B has now tested whether the current archive can recover that missing stimulus. The answer splits cleanly. A strong photographic proxy exists: the official archive contains time-correlated Glushko and Ohm frames with exactly matched Nikon D5, lens, 400 mm, f/8, ISO 400, and 0.001 s state, plus a wider frame containing both craters. The three labels preserve 976, 1,408, and 801 fitted pixel-to-lunar-coordinate control points, and all three images visibly contain their coordinate-localized ray craters. But the actual human stimulus remains unrecoverable: the labels do not provide a complete spacecraft/lighting solution, current SPICE is still listed as `TBD`, and the record does not fix naked-eye versus viewfinder/display mediation or recover window and visual-adaptation state. Stage 8B admits one camera-native paired-and-joint-image canary; it cannot establish Hansen's perception.

Stage 8C executes that canary and closes the lane. The primary joint-frame AUCs are `0.537977` for Glushko and `0.497915` for Ohm; the matched-pair values are `0.529397` and `0.500062`. All `6 / 6` cells preserve the Glushko-greater-than-Ohm direction, but `0 / 6` pass because the two-target signal and similarity requirements fail. That is a bounded direction-only residual, not concordance with the compound “very similar, Glushko slightly brighter” statement. Alternate exposures, hand-drawn regions, channel changes, and threshold repair remain excluded.

Stage 9A then widens the aperture instead of repairing that failed lane. Eight archive families were compared with truthful synthesis and deliberate stop. The strongest bounded question is the gray feature Koch reported at `23:22:39 UTC` immediately before/during Earthrise. Wiseman proposed mountains; Koch allowed optical illusion; NASA calls it a potential sighting and states that no lofted dust or exospheric glow was definitively detected. The repository already holds SAW Cam 3 frames at `23:22:14`, `23:22:44`, and `23:23:14 UTC`, but Stage 9A deliberately opened no new candidate pixels. It selects a source/control test, not a dust result.

Stage 9B finds a surprisingly strong internal carrier and a decisive control failure. The exact exchange is in PCD3 product `art002a000015`, not the overlapping PCD2 transcript in the original corpus. A bounded archive query returns 40 D5 raw frames across `23:22:37.530–23:22:49.330 UTC`; five exposure-bracket frames begin only `0.020–0.390 s` after Koch's `23:22:39.010 UTC` report. Yet the same recording documents persistent cabin reflections, the labels do not bind the exact operator/eye/window path, the window stack lacks an event-specific optical model, and exact geometry is incomplete. The sole near-time external frame is a different-viewpoint GoPro exposure at `+4.990 s`, ISO 100, about `0.000384 s`, and -2 EV with no demonstrated faint-source sensitivity equivalence. Stage 9B therefore closes the image lane as `SOURCE_CONTROL_INSUFFICIENT` without opening any gray-feature pixels.

The earlier operational result remains valid as supporting evidence: the observation timeline is a coordination surface whose fields cannot be treated as physical-event clocks, and actual execution cannot be reconstructed from schedule fields or product labels alone.

That conclusion survives every completed check. Earthrise and Earthset have opposite availability-window relations under the same fixed-camera method, while all 30 numbered plan entries have bounded execution classifications. Twenty-one have exact title or recorder anchors, eight more have direct execution evidence, and only the Forward Look discussion remains unresolved. Nothing in the pinned evidence proves a target was missed or diverted.

The impact flashes are no longer an underreported Testbed lead. No inspected pixel in this repository independently reproduces that confirmation. ## The evidence ladder

The archive becomes intelligible only when four evidence layers stay separate:

| Layer | What it can establish | What it cannot establish here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan and schedule metadata | Intended target, request time, nominal duration, available window | Exact crew entry, physical contact time, completion, displacement |
| Crew transcript | Spoken transition, active observation, description, recovery, attention choice | Unspoken activity, exact timing across gaps, physical truth of a reported phenomenon |
| Product labels | Product identity, acquisition time, payload burden, declared activity/target tags | Pointing, visible content, correct activity attribution, scientific validation |
| Pixels, geometry, or instrument corroboration | Potential visual or physical confirmation when independently qualified | The Testbed inspected bounded LOLA and WAC rasters plus three exact processed camera PNGs for Stages 7A, 8A, and 8C, but did not inspect flash or gray-feature pixels or reproduce NASA's confirmation |
| Current primary mission findings | NASA's published conclusions and source-exclusion work | Independent reproduction by this Testbed |

The reusable lesson is simple: **plan → conversation → capture → validation is an evidence ladder, not a chain of automatic conversions.** Most false conclusions in this archive come from skipping a rung.

## Ranked findings

### 1. One held-out language-to-LOLA signal survives, but the first cross-axis reflectance test does not

**Confidence: high for the measured directional concordance; low that the current pair isolates a distinct human-information mechanism.**

NASA's preliminary report makes a useful qualitative cross-instrument comparison. Around Orientale, the crew described broad changes using terms such as softer, looser, fresh-powder-looking, mossy, hummocky, and pillowy. NASA says one textural change is apparent in a LOLA-derived roughness map but not in the Diviner-derived rock-abundance map, suggesting a relationship to hectometer-scale terrain properties rather than 1–2 m rocks.

Specialist methodology changes what follows. A LOLA roughness baseline is the separation over which slope or height deviation is computed. The map then aggregates those measurements over regional windows measured in kilometers. The visible regional pattern, the measurement baseline, and an individual recognized object's size are different quantities. Dividing 0.1 km by the report's separate 1–6 km rille-recognition range does not measure how far below resolution human perception reached.

The rille result is also task-specific. NASA explicitly says landmark recognition varied with range, viewing angle, illumination, and competing bright or colored objects, and that brightness could matter more than physical size. Controlled sub-resolution line-texture psychophysics cannot close that transfer gap.

Stage 7A performs the first pre-instrument check outside Orientale. In the Vavilov transcript, Glover contrasts rocky, broken, jagged and rough terrain with the smooth, glossy look of Hertzsprung. The direction was frozen before LOLA value access. Equal-size target-centered regions then showed materially higher median slope at Vavilov across the 57 m, 225 m, and 560 m products, with 9/9 radius-and-scale comparisons retaining the expected ordering.

This moves the project from “plausible test” to “coarse signal worth discriminating.” It does not yet show semantic information beyond crater morphology. Vavilov and Hertzsprung are an easy contrast; one observer produced all descriptions; and exact viewing footprints were not recovered. The result is not statistical significance because the raster values are interpolated from overlapping windows and are not independent samples.

Stage 8A supplies the first real generalization pressure. Hansen directly compared Glushko and Ohm rays, so the claim was frozen separately from the roughness result and scored against one symmetric local-contrast endpoint. It failed completely: both named sectors are darker than their flanks, the predicted ordinal direction reverses, and all nine robustness cells reject the full oracle. Raw scene medians cannot be substituted after the fact because the frozen claim concerned ray-to-local-background contrast, not global tile brightness.

Stage 8C answers that remaining camera-native question. It freezes the joint frame as the primary same-exposure comparison, the matched 400 mm pair as secondary, three whole-annulus region cells derived from official coordinates and label control points, and a rank endpoint over uncalibrated RGB palette code values. The primary joint-frame AUCs are `0.537977` for Glushko and `0.497915` for Ohm; the matched-pair values are `0.529397` and `0.500062`. All six cells preserve the Glushko-greater-than-Ohm direction, but none satisfies the complete two-target signal and similarity oracle.

The useful inference is narrower than “astronaut language does not match instruments.” The Stage 7A agreement may be driven by obvious crater morphology. Both a prospectively frozen static representation and the exact available camera representation fail to reproduce Hansen's compound Glushko/Ohm statement under their qualified local-contrast endpoints. The recurring direction alone is not enough: Ohm lacks the required local signal in the primary cells, and the effect sizes are not similar. The exact archive-supported Glushko/Ohm lane therefore stops without alternate exposures, redrawn regions, channel switching, or threshold repair.

**What would change this:** an independently justified human-stimulus reconstruction or calibrated physical model could ask a genuinely different question. An actual SPICE release may strengthen geometry, but it cannot retroactively turn processed camera code values into Hansen's perception or reopen the frozen Stage 8C oracle.

### 2. The observation timeline is a coordination surface, not a physical-event clock

**Confidence: high for the tested semantics; medium for mission-wide recurrence.**

NASA’s own description treats the relevant OTR fields as observation-availability windows. Earthrise remains the clearest hard negative to endpoint equivalence: its official window lasts 4.147844 seconds, while the crew’s first-corner-to-full-clear progression lasts 128.213 seconds. A fixed-camera sequence independently places full clearance at least 136 seconds before that window begins.

Stage 2B prevents overgeneralization. Earthset's fixed-camera occultation overlaps its availability window for at least 54.269803 seconds. Sunset ends before full occultation and Sunrise cannot supply a post-clearance stability frame, so both stop rather than being widened after inspection. Earthset is one admissible counterexample to uniform non-overlap, not evidence of a recurring alternative pattern.

Stage 2C adds a sharply bounded explanation candidate. NASA's operations report compares an actual view at `22:33:56` with a post-TLI visualization at `22:36:30`, documenting a 154-second earlier realization. A bounded publication research pass found a second matched-view comparison in the same report: a 400 mm crew image at `20:12:20` beside an LTP simulation for `20:15:00`, a 160-second difference. Both values fall inside Earthrise's independently measured `[136, 166)`-second full-clearance-to-window-start lag. Earthset's 196.650033-second availability window is 47.41 times wider than Earthrise's 4.147844-second window, so an offset of that scale can leave Earthset overlapping while separating Earthrise completely.

This is a falsifiable trajectory-offset/window-tolerance hypothesis, not a confirmed cause. The two comparisons make the documented offset scale less dependent on one image pair, but they do not establish that the offset was constant, that every OTR window inherited it, or that the planning process failed. The direct crew transcript also matters: Earthrise began and finished before the four-second interval, while the broader 12-minute requested activity slot covered the event. The safe interpretation is layered operational resilience, not a missed observation.

The important reversal is not that the crew was “early.” The fields and the spoken optical events are different kinds of time. Calling their difference a delay would manufacture a scheduling result from a semantic mismatch.

**What would change this:** the delayed Artemis II SPICE bundle can test event-specific actual trajectory geometry. The hypothesis fails if Earthrise's actual-versus-planned shift lies outside the frozen 136-to-166-second lag or if its OTR timing did not inherit the documented post-TLI planning basis.

### 3. The crew executed the plan more completely than exact-title matching suggests

**Confidence: high for the bounded ledger; not a completion or quality score.**

Stage 3A found 21 exact target-entry cues. A naive analysis would leave nine gaps. Close transcript review recovered direct evidence for eight of them:

- two regional color/albedo discussions began through subject matter rather than spoken plan titles;
- an Impact Flashes search occurred before its recorder cue and ended with an explicit negative observation;
- two Eclipsed Moon and Deep Space pairs ran concurrently or continued across a transcript gap; and
- Sunrise began with explicit corona orientation, followed by streamer and solar-emergence reports.

The remaining Forward Look discussion is unresolved because the planned window contains other real work but no distinct synthesis of flyby observations or future Artemis science operations. Keeping that state unresolved is more informative than forcing either success or failure.

**What would change this:** a missing transcript, recorder, annotation, or official activity record that directly establishes the Forward Look discussion. An image activity label by itself does not.

### 4. Artemis II science execution was adaptive and parallel, not merely “on time” or “off plan”

**Confidence: strong as a situated interpretation of the pinned sequence.**

The transcripts contain explicit recovery and attention control:

- an unplugged headset was followed by voice-recorder and written-note recovery;
- Glover named the terminator as a tempting alternative and deliberately returned attention to Vavilov;
- later terminator work occurred inside a Crew Choice period, where deviation was expected; and
- simultaneous Earthshine and Deep Space entries have distinct content evidence rather than one merged activity.

This is the deeper operational picture: the plan supplied targets and windows, while the crew negotiated attention, equipment, visibility, documentation, and unexpected phenomena in real time. A binary compliance model throws away the most useful behavior in the record.

**What would change this:** broader transcript analysis could show that these episodes were exceptional rather than representative. The current evidence supports this Flight Day 6 sequence, not all Artemis operations.

### 5. NASA's confirmed impact flashes are published prior art, not this Testbed's discovery lead

**Confidence: high that NASA currently classifies the five sightings as impact flashes; independent Testbed reproduction is absent.**

Inside the nominal Forward Look window, Wiseman reported a pinpoint event, Hansen confirmed that both had seen the same thing, and the crew described a small flash on the Moon. The Testbed independently reconstructed that transcript episode and identified contemporaneous capture availability.

The archive also contains potentially relevant capture coverage:

- two SAW3 channel products span the complete report interval;
- five Nikon Z9 raw exposures begin from `00:51:51.83Z` through `00:51:56.10Z`; and

### 6. The immediate media bottleneck is extraction architecture, not server access

**Confidence: high for the tested route; deliberately narrow.**
